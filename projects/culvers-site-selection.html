Culver's Canadian Growth Strategy — Site Selection

A mock market-entry study scoring candidate trade areas on demographics, accessibility, and competitor saturation to rank where Culver's should open first.

Tools ArcGIS StoryMaps ArcGIS Pro FME Workbench Excel

TypeSite selection
GeographySouthern Ontario
OutputArcGIS StoryMap

Objective

A mock market-entry exercise: if Culver's were expanding into Canada, where should the first locations go? The analysis scores candidate areas on customer fit, accessibility, and competitive pressure, then ranks them into a recommended opening sequence.

Data

  • Demographics — Statistics Canada census variables: population, household income, age structure, household size.
  • Competitors — QSR locations from ChainXY, used to measure saturation.
  • Traffic & accessibility — major road network and traffic indicators.
  • Analysis grid — hexagonal tessellation covering the study area, used as the scoring unit.

Method

  1. Define the customer profile — translated Culver's U.S. trade-area characteristics into measurable Canadian census variables.
  2. Build the analysis grid — generated a hex tessellation so every candidate area is compared on an equal-sized unit, avoiding the distortion you get from irregular census geography.
  3. Enrich the grid — apportioned demographic variables onto each hex.
  4. Score competition — counted and distance-weighted competitor QSR locations per hex to capture saturation and proximity.
  5. Weighted suitability model — normalised each input and combined them into a single composite score, weighted by how much each factor drives Culver's performance.
  6. Rank and shortlist — ranked hexes, clustered the top performers into candidate trade areas, and sequenced them into a phased entry recommendation.

Outcome & takeaway

The study produces a defensible shortlist with the reasoning visible at every step — which is the point of a story map rather than a static deck. The weighting is transparent and adjustable, so a stakeholder who disagrees with an assumption can see exactly what changes if it's reweighted. This is the same framework used for real retail client expansion work, applied to a brand with no Canadian footprint.
